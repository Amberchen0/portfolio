Design Concept. 设 计 陈 述

Statement · 陈述

This streetscape is a corner of the city at dusk—the hour when people stop containing what they feel and let the night carry it. I built it as an observer: a stranger watching strangers, making no claim to know who they are. The purple building stays still, a quiet witness, while faceless figures drift through ordinary acts: resting under a lamp, taking out trash, smoking in a doorway, arguing after a crash, dancing on a balcony.

By daylight, the violet façade wears the readable surface of identity, and the inward-looking windows hold a quiet spectrum of emotion. When night arrives, the patterned motifs ignite into neon and the interior glows outward—the city sheds its social shell and lets emotion speak in saturated color. Architecture becomes the body of identity; light becomes the language of emotion.

I did not know any of these people. I gave them the colors I felt while watching them. Color, here, is not a label but a reading—mine. It is also visibility: some figures are loud and vivid, asking to be seen; others fade into the darker end of the spectrum and almost disappear into the night. To wear a color is to choose how much of yourself the city is allowed to read.

The viewer may read differently. What I sensed as restlessness might appear, to someone else, as longing, or fever, or love. This work does not insist on a single answer—it records one observer’s moment of inner translation, and leaves room for every other.

这是黄昏时分城市的一处街角。夜色漫上来，人们松开白天紧攥着的情绪，把它们交给夜晚去承接。我以一个旁观者的身份做了这件作品：一个陌生人，看着另一群陌生人，并不声称自己理解他们。紫色的楼安静地立在那里，像一个沉默的见证者；几个没有面孔的人物在它周围做着最普通的动作——倚在路灯下、倒垃圾、在门口抽烟、为一起车祸对骂、在阳台上跳舞。

白天，紫色的立面是这栋楼给路人看的那张脸，朝内的窗户里收着一片更安静的情绪；夜色一来，墙上的图案点亮成霓虹，建筑开始从内部向外发光——城市卸下它的社交外壳，让情绪用饱和的颜色说话。建筑成为身份的躯体，光成为情绪的语言。

我并不认识这些人中的任何一个。我把看他们时自己产生的情绪——用颜色——还给了他们。颜色在这里不是一个标签，而是一个解读，属于我的解读。它同时也是一种"可见度"：有人鲜亮张扬，希望被看见；另一些人则用深色把自己折进夜里，几乎隐形。给自己穿上一种颜色，也就是在决定：愿意让这座城市读出多少自己。

观众未必和我读到同一个答案。我画成烦躁的人，在别人眼里也许是渴望，是燥热，或是恋爱。我并不打算给出唯一的答案——这件作品只是记录了一个旁观者一刻的内心转译，剩下的部分，留给每一个看它的人。

How it Began

Before the model, there were a painting, a notebook, and a stack of sheets — a slow gathering of cities, lights, and figures that would later live inside.
