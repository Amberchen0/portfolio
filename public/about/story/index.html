About · A Self-Portrait

Drawing is my way of understanding the world.

More than a decade of artistic study and practice have made images my natural way of observing and recording — emotions, memories, and the quiet bonds between people that language can't quite hold. Before I learned to explain things, I had already learned to draw them.

For me, every act of creation is a complete cognitive experiment. I begin with a feeling, search for the shape that fits it, and then — through colour, structure, and rhythm — return to read the feeling back. The making itself is how I understand.

If drawing is my language, my bachelor's at the University of Technology Sydney taught me how to speak it — figure, mood, light; the patience of working frame by frame. The Master of Global Media Communication at the University of Melbourne didn't replace any of that. It broadened the dimensions through which I understand the world: narrative, audience, the way an image travels across platforms and cultures.

Studying overseas and working across disciplines slowly turned me from a quiet observer into someone who reaches outward. Brand visual, AI-augmented production, art department on Australian short-drama sets, soft furnishing for pre-sale homes, independent projects — each one a different grammar for the same instinct. I learned that an idea only becomes real when it lands inside someone else.

I love creating not only because it lets me say something, but because it keeps me growing. Each project is, in some quiet way, a re-finding of myself — a chance to test what I notice, what I care about, and what I still want to change about how I see.
